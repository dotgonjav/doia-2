About the project

A family wanted to cook, eat and spend time together in one room, without that one room becoming restless. The answer lay in the ger itself: the nomadic tent with a single room, where everything happens around a fixed centre. Here that centre is a kitchen made from one block of pale travertine, with dining table and lounge arranged around it along the windows.

The kitchen stands as a freestanding volume within the space. Tall cabinets in dark smoked oak form the back wall; in front of them stands the island, fully clad in travertine, with a sink cut from the same stone. Storage and services disappear behind the cabinet wall, so the living space itself stays empty and calm.

Floor-to-ceiling windows run along the facade, behind linen curtains that soften the light. Dining table and lounge each sit on their own wool rug, as two places on one continuous oak floor. Every moment of the day gets a fixed place, without a single dividing wall.

About the materials

Walls and ceiling are finished in lime plaster, light and matte, with a clouded texture that spreads the daylight softly. The floor is wide oak plank in a warm grey-brown tone and runs without interruption from kitchen to lounge.

The travertine is pale and vein-cut, so its natural veins run as fine horizontal lines across island, worktop and splashback. Against it stands the smoked oak of the tall cabinets: a calm, almost black plane that anchors the kitchen. A curved tap in brushed steel is the only metal on the island.

The furniture speaks the same language: a coffee table in solid dark timber, built up like stacked beams, black spindle chairs around a long dark table, benches and armchairs in teak with cane seats, a stool cut from a single piece of raw wood. Ceramics, old terracotta jars and art in deep blue and moss green bring colour without overpowering the whole.
