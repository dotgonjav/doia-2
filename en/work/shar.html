About the project

A sculptor was looking for a home close to his studio. The answer lay in a protected old warehouse in raw concrete, housing the studio on the ground floor and, through a new intervention, a dwelling above. The existing shell was to remain all but untouched.

That called for a construction that can be removed without leaving traces: a demountable steel structure, free of the shell, carries the stair, the floors and the balustrades of the new living space.

Function is read through colour. The load-bearing structure stands entirely in soft blue, the inhabited core, kitchen and bathroom, is executed in yellow mortex. Blue carries, yellow inhabits.

About the materials

The existing shell was left as it was: vertical board-formed concrete, raw and earthen, with the grain of the planks still visible. Against it the new intervention reads as something of its own: placed free, reversible, recognisable by colour. The steel structure and spiral stair are powder-coated in a single soft blue; the stair turns as a light spiral through the double height, carried by oak joists for the upper floor.

The board-formed concrete of the warehouse remained exactly as it was, raw, with the grain of the planks still visible. Against it the new intervention stands as something recognisably other: continuous steel profiles carrying the stair, the floors and the balustrades, which could in theory be removed again without damaging the concrete.

The inhabited core is finished in yellow mortex, a mineral, seamless skin that binds kitchen and bathroom into one whole, in sharp contrast with the surrounding concrete and steel.
