About the project

On the left bank, where city and nature just touch, a couple wanted a place to truly withdraw without leaving the city. Baigal is built from discarded shipping containers, a second life for a structure that would otherwise be demolished.

The brief was intimacy: not a large, representative space, but a stay that closes around two people. Vertical blackened wood, from floor to ceiling, does exactly that, it absorbs the light and draws the eye inward.

A single window breaks that restraint open and brings the landscape inside. Sustainability here is not a label, but the choice to reuse what was already standing.

About the materials

Placed vertically, blackened wood, in the spirit of shou sugi ban, clads walls, ceiling and the built-in furniture, and sets the calm, restrained tone of the stay. Against it stands red travertine, fiery and energetic: the contrast is deliberately sharp.

That contrast does more than show; it points the way. Where the wood lets the space rest, the stone marks the function, worktop, basin, splashback, and at once sets the mood of that place. An oak floor and linen cushions soften the whole.
